# Evidence-led content strategy

## Decision

Build one recognizable channel promise: **visual explanations of invisible systems,
dangerous failures, and strange true mechanisms**. This is broader than “history,”
but narrow enough that a viewer who likes one video has a reason to watch the next.

The system must not imitate another creator's wording, visuals, or identity. It uses
repeatable audience patterns—consequence-first hooks, escalating reveals, clear
mechanism animation, and factual payoff—to create original work from primary and
reputable sources.

## What the channel evidence says

- The established Shorts audience responds to immediate dramatic scenes. The best
  audited Shorts reached 67.3K and 33.2K views; the latter held 90.8% average viewed.
- The recent automated factual/cinematic series was much weaker. A 1,019-view Short
  held only 49.8% stayed-to-watch and 50.8% average viewed.
- Among the small non-music 16:9 sample, the consequence-led DHIS2/malaria video
  retained 52% at 30 seconds, versus 32% for a slower Google Sheets tutorial.
- Search currently matters for 16:9 videos, but the engine should design for browse
  and suggested growth, not produce keyword-stuffed tutorials.

Music lyric uploads are excluded from concept selection. Their audience geography
may later inform language, cultural context, and upload timing, but they are not a
creative template for the explainer engine.

## External patterns worth adopting

Million-view explainer and documentary channels repeatedly package a concrete
subject with a high-stakes unanswered question. Examples include fern's quantified
objects and crimes, Fascinating Horror's cause-plus-consequence disaster titles,
RealLifeLore's counterintuitive geography, Astrum's real-footage space explanations,
TED-Ed's body-mechanism questions, and neo's preventable-failure documentaries.

What repeats across videos—not just isolated hits:

1. Show the consequence before explaining the setup.
2. Make the title promise one specific answer.
3. Make the thumbnail ask a related visual question; never duplicate the title.
4. Introduce a new fact, implication, location, or visual model every 8–20 seconds.
5. Preserve one open question through the middle, then deliver a concrete payoff.
6. Prefer evidence that can be visualized: maps, timelines, diagrams, scale, anatomy,
   archival records, satellite imagery, and object-level mechanisms.
7. End on implication or reversal, not a generic subscribe request.

## Content pillars

| Pillar | Repeatable promise | Visual language | Revenue fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Invisible failures | The tiny fault behind a huge consequence | cutaways, diagrams, timelines | engineering/technology |
| Body under pressure | What the body does in extreme conditions | anatomy, particles, thermals | health/science |
| Strange science in real life | An unseen mechanism changes an ordinary system | simulations, macro views | science/technology |
| Maps and systems | Why geography or infrastructure creates an outcome | maps, scale comparisons | education/business |

## Format specifications

### Shorts (45–70 seconds)

- 0–2 s: visible consequence plus a precise claim.
- 2–8 s: identity/context; no greeting or channel intro.
- 8–35 s: mechanism in escalating steps; visual change roughly every 1.5–3 s.
- 35–55 s: reversal or missing fact.
- Final 5–10 s: payoff that resolves the first frame and can loop naturally.
- 105–175 spoken words; captions limited to short semantic phrases.
- Primary success gates: stayed-to-watch, engaged views, average percentage viewed,
  replays, shares, and subscribers per 1,000 views.

### 16:9 (3.5–5 minutes)

- 0–6 s: cold open with consequence and unanswered mechanism.
- 6–25 s: promise, stakes, and why the viewer should care now.
- 25–90 s: establish the system and first causal link.
- 90–210 s: escalation through 2–3 discoveries or decisions.
- 210–270 s: reveal/payoff and the clearest visual model.
- Final 15–30 s: implication, unresolved risk, or useful conclusion.
- 525–825 spoken words; visual change or meaningful reframing within 5–10 s.
- Primary success gates: thumbnail impressions CTR, 30-second retention, average
  percentage viewed, end-screen continuation, subscribers, and RPM.

## Initial proof pair

Use one researched topic in two forms to lower research and asset cost:

- Short working promise: **“A Particle From Space Can Flip a Computer Bit.”**
- 16:9 working title: **“The Invisible Particles That Quietly Break Computers.”**
- Thumbnail question: a glowing particle crossing a chip with one corrupted red bit;
  optional text: **ONE FLIPPED BIT**.

This is not a script approval. Production begins only after the research packet has
primary sources, claim-level citations, rights-cleared visual options, and a concept
score of at least 78/100.

## Production rules

- Facts require claim-level sources; sensitive claims require corroboration.
- Use public-domain material, licensed stock, original graphics, or generated stills.
- Never use unlicensed movie clips as the owned-system foundation.
- Voice should be calm, assured, and human-paced; urgency comes from writing and
  sound design, not shouting.
- Music supports tension at low level and ducks under narration. Use licensed or
  original tracks only. Add purposeful impacts, risers, ambience, and silence.
- Descriptions lead with the viewer payoff, summarize the mechanism, then list
  sources. Tags are descriptive metadata, not a growth strategy.
- Nothing uploads automatically until factual, visual, audio, and copyright QA pass.

## Cost target for the proof engine

| Component | MVP choice | Typical variable cost |
|---|---|---:|
| Research, scoring, scripts, plans | ChatGPT/Codex workflow | existing subscription |
| Editing/render | FFmpeg locally/GitHub runner | $0 |
| Stock | public domain + free licensed sources | $0 |
| Graphics | original programmatic diagrams + selective generated stills | $0–$2/video |
| Voice | provider adapter or local model | $0–$1/video |
| Music/SFX | free licensed library | $0 |
| Thumbnail | original layout + selective generated image | $0–$1/video |

The target is **under $1 per Short and under $5 per five-minute video** before any
optional premium generation. Budget gates live in every project manifest.
