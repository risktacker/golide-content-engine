He saw gaps in a railroad every 39 feet,
and 2 companies are racing to build a railroad along a canyon.
Whoever finishes first gets the whole country’s business.
And when his boss sees gaps between every single rail, his face goes purple:
You’re wrecking my railroad.
But he won’t close a single one.
So the other company lays their rails end to end, perfectly smooth,
and finishes first.
Their trains glide across the canyon without a sound,
while his go clickety-clack over every gap,
and the newspapers start calling it the clickety-clack line.
Then summer comes, and something weird happens.
His gaps start shrinking.
In May, you can fit a coin in them.
By July, barely a sheet of paper.
Meanwhile, on the perfect railroad, the rails have started groaning in the afternoon sun,
pushing against each other like they want to get out.
And on the hottest day anyone can remember, his gaps finally close,
rail touching rail.
And halfway across the canyon, the perfect railroad kicks sideways like a snake,
right as a train rolls onto it.
Follow the channel for more.
