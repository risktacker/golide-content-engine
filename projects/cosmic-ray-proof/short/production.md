# Short production brief

## Voice

- Neutral international English; warm, intelligent, quietly fascinated.
- 168–174 WPM; no trailer voice, shouting, fake suspense, or exaggerated pauses.
- Emphasise: “without,” “one stored bit,” “soft error,” and the final contrast.

## Sound

- Hook: dry digital tick; no music for first 0.4 seconds.
- Atmospheric cascade: soft granular spread.
- Bit flip: 200 ms silence, then one clean electrical click.
- ECC repair: two ascending confirmation tones.
- Music: restrained curiosity bed at roughly -24 LUFS under narration.

## Captions

- Two lines maximum; 2–5 words per semantic phrase.
- Off-white type with red only on the changed bit or danger word.
- Never highlight every spoken word karaoke-style.

## Metadata

**Description:** A high-energy particle interaction can create a temporary electrical
charge inside a memory chip and change stored data. Here is how single-event upsets
work—and how computers detect them. Sources: ESA, NASA, IBM and IEEE.

**Tags:** cosmic rays, bit flip, single event upset, computer memory, soft error,
space radiation, science explained
