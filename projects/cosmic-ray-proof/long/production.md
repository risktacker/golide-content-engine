# Long-form production brief

## Voice and sound

- Neutral international English, 148–154 WPM, calm and conversational.
- Curiosity rises through meaning, not theatrical delivery.
- Sound motifs: paper movement, faint particle hiss, clean bit click, restrained
  electronic pulse. Music changes only at mechanism, Swarm evidence, and correction.
- Narration integrated target: approximately -16 LUFS; music kept clearly below it.

## Thumbnail

- One large hand-drawn chip fills roughly 55% of the frame.
- A bright cyan particle enters from upper left and terminates at one red memory cell.
- The cell shows a large 0 turning into 1; no computer monitor, astronaut, face, or
  generic space background.
- Optional text: **ONE FLIPPED BIT**. Test a no-text version first.
- Title supplies the cause; thumbnail supplies the visible mystery.

## Description

A computer can store the wrong value even when its hardware still works. This video
explains how energetic particles create single-event upsets, what ESA observed in a
decade of Swarm spacecraft data, and how error-correcting memory repairs many bit
flips automatically.

Sources include ESA, NASA, IBM Research and IEEE. Full citations are listed in the
project research packet.
